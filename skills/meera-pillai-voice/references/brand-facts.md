## About Meera
- Founder of Skinstinct, an Indian direct-to-consumer skincare brand.
- Spent two years in pharmaceutical formulation before founding the brand.
- Not a dermatologist; no medical degree. Can read a CoA and understands stability documentation and ingredient-system behaviour.
- Founding moment: in 2021, at a stability review meeting at her pharma employer, a cosmeceutical at pH 3.2 carried a 24-month stability claim despite the active degrading below pH 3.8; the test protocol had been inherited from a different product. Nobody else asked. It passed review. She left 7 months later.
- Founding rationale in her words: a documentation gap and a decision to do something about it.

## Formulation and manufacturing standards
- Brief to contract manufacturer requires pH documentation, stability testing at three-month intervals for the first year, and mid-batch CoA sampling.
- pH is documented on every batch.
- The serum (niacinamide-based) has pH 5.5-5.8 (one newsletter cites 5.5-6.0 as an illustrative example; prefer 5.5-5.8).
- Spent several months adjusting the serum base until CoA results matched application consistency tests; label percentage unchanged throughout.
- Everything is fragrance-free, including preservative system, emollients and carrier ingredients.
- No parabens, for pragmatic and customer-trust reasons, not because she believes they are dangerous at cosmetic concentrations. She says this openly.
- Ceramide approach: ceramide NP and AP, paired with cholesterol and fatty acids to approximate the physiological ratio. No product is currently marketed primarily on barrier-repair claims.
- Publishes on the website exactly what product testing covers: sample size, study type, who ran it. Some testing is early stage.
- Short INCI compared with most competitors.

## Products: what they do NOT sell (as of the source content)
- No Vitamin C product. Has been trying to formulate one for about 14 months; challenges are L-ascorbic acid stability above 10% (needs airless pump, pH below 3.5, antioxidant support system), cost for their price range, and humid-climate base.
- No peptide product.
- No sunscreen.
- Two products currently in formulation, not discussed in detail. One has been in development over a year.

## Business data (only use these exact figures)
- 12 months to June 2025: 23% of returns cited texture/feel; 71% of those were from cities with average annual humidity above 70% (Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi, Kolkata).
- Original serum base was too film-forming for high humidity. Reformulated with a lighter base and adjusted humectant-to-occlusive balance; returns from humid cities dropped to 8% the following quarter; active concentrations unchanged.
- Return rate in high-humidity cities had been almost three times that elsewhere.
- Repeat purchase rate at 12 months: 67% (vs 20-40% typical she cites for DTC skincare).
- Has explained the fragrance-free decision "a few hundred times".

## Recurring positions she holds
- The label percentage is the beginning of the question, not the answer; pH, delivery base and batch process decide what's actually delivered.
- "Clinically tested", "natural" and "clean" are not meaningfully regulated claims in India; ask brands in writing what they mean.
- Climate: base formulations built for European/Korean markets often don't suit Indian humidity; ask manufacturers what climate a base was validated for.
- SPF: most people apply 0.5-1mg/cm² versus the 2mg/cm² test standard; SPF in moisturiser is not a substitute for standalone sunscreen; reapply every two hours outdoors.
- Natural vs synthetic is an unproductive binary.
- Parabens: evidence of harm at cosmetic concentrations is essentially absent; some replacement preservatives cause more sensitisation.
- Fragrance: highest contact-sensitisation category; sensitisation is cumulative and permanent; aesthetic value doesn't justify the risk.
- The newsletter list is "not going to be a place where I sell you things"; product emails will explain exactly what's in the product and why.
